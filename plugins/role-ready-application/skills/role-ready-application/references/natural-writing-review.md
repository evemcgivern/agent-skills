# Natural-writing and candidate-voice review

## Purpose and limit

Help the candidate make application writing clearer, more specific, and recognizably theirs. This is an editorial review, not an AI detector, authorship test, or promise of detector evasion. Do not promise that text will “pass” a detector, hide AI use, or optimize for detector scores.

## Inputs

Use the current candidate's resume, matching job description, relevant cover-letter draft, and only the candidate's explicitly provided writing sample or voice preferences. A writing sample is optional. Do not use the creator's style, another writer's voice, a prior candidate, unrelated history, or a universal list of “human” mannerisms as the target.

If no sample or preference is available, preserve the candidate's existing wording, dialect, and register. Distinctive voice assessment may be UNVERIFIED, but clarity review can proceed. Say when voice cannot be assessed confidently. Do not infer personality, values, or motivations from prose.

## Review method

1. Check factual support and qualifiers before style. Preserve claims, evidence, technical terms, uncertainty, responsibility level, chronology, and attribution exactly. A style edit that changes factual meaning is a failure.
2. Look for specific passages that are generic, over-smoothed, repetitive, inflated, cliché, or unsupported by the candidate's concrete evidence. Do not mark ordinary or formal language as a problem merely because it is common.
3. Cite each finding by section and short passage. Explain its reader effect in one sentence.
4. Offer a minimal edit using candidate-supplied language or evidence. In an authorized editing task, apply meaning-preserving edits and mark unresolved decisions; in a standalone review, present suggestions without silently changing the draft. When missing specificity or a view belongs to the candidate, ask a neutral question instead of writing it for them.
5. Remove filler only when facts, nuance, and voice remain intact. Preserve technical vocabulary even when it is uncommon. Do not replace accurate terms with generic synonyms.
6. Treat dialect, regional spelling, and meaningful grammar choices as voice evidence unless the candidate asks for a specific house style or the choice blocks comprehension.
7. Separate factual blockers from optional voice choices. Do not silently rewrite a standalone review. In an authorized editing task, apply only changes that preserve meaning and clearly mark unresolved candidate decisions.
8. Make one bounded revision and recheck affected claims. Do not loop until prose appears “human.”

## Output

Return **PASS**, **NEEDS REVISION**, or **UNVERIFIED**, and state the scope reviewed. For findings, use:

| Location / phrase | Issue and evidence | Minimal suggestion or neutral question | Candidate decision |
|---|---|---|---|

Use “None identified” when there are no findings. Use **UNVERIFIED** where a claim or voice judgment lacks needed evidence. A successful style review does not establish factual accuracy, document pagination, or detector outcome.
